# Safi Ullah — Statement and Self-Reflection

## Statement

For this assignment, my assigned role was to examine cluster location and
movement for signs that some clusters might have a different origin. I joined
Harris Parts I and III by cluster ID and checked that the result still
contained 157 clusters. Of these, 143 had the movement measurement needed
for my plots; the other 14 did not. I plotted movement against galactic
longitude, compared six longitude sections in 60-degree increments, and
flagged the 10% with the largest movement magnitudes. I repeated the
flagging at 5% and 15% to see how dependent the shortlist was on my chosen
cutoff, then exported the results for the combined analysis.

I also contributed to the age-metallicity work. Aditi had made the Harris
Part II plots, but the linked data file was not being read properly, so I
made a notebook that converted its fixed-width layout into a CSV her
notebook could use. I then completed the remaining age analysis in that
notebook using Daniel's merged table. I kept the van den Berg and Krause
measurements separate rather than averaging disagreements. For each
cluster, I compared its age with the median age of at least four others
of similar metallicity, flagged differences of at least 2 billion years,
and repeated the test with different settings.

Finally, I connected the saved age and movement results in my holistic
notebook. NGC 1851 was flagged by both screens. Pal 12 stood out in the
age analysis but not in the movement screen, while NGC 6171's age flag
was less stable when the settings changed. I kept "not flagged" separate
from "not enough data to assess."

## Self-reflection

Okay, so the main lesson for me was that real data requires decisions
the dataset cannot make for us. Fourteen clusters did not have the
movement value I needed, the two age sources sometimes disagreed, and
neither a 10% cutoff nor a 2-billion-year difference is a universal
boundary. Plotting helped me notice unusual points, but applying a
stated rule and changing its settings showed which results were more
stable. The combined analysis let me identify and rank candidates,
while also showing why those measurements alone cannot establish where
a cluster formed.

I was also acting as a coordinator. When a teammate became ill, she
still attended meetings and contributed plots, but the final age
analysis remained unfinished close to submission, so I completed it and
connected it to the rest of the project. I learned that checking
progress earlier, setting clearer internal deadlines, and preparing a
fallback are as important as making the initial plan. Next time, I
would ask sooner in meetings what help someone needs when their work
stalls.
