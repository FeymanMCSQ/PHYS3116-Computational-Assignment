## Self Reflection
My main role was merging the Harris and van den Bergh catalogues into a master table for the team. The biggest challenge was figuring out how to match the catalogues, because each had their own unique way of naming clusters. For example, Harris named clusters as "NGC 104", whilst van den Bergh named it "104" and Krause21 named it "NGC104". I addressed this by creating a function which extracted the numbers from the IDs, and then reformatted them to match the Harris-style format. For example, "NGC104" and "104" both got extracted to "104", and then rebuilt to "NGC 104". This ensured that the right clusters were matching. Another small issue at the beginning of the project was deciding if Krause21 was to be used or not. The Moodle page provided Krause21 as a data source. However, the project brief only mentioned the Harris and van den Bergh catalogues. To address this, I only used Krause21 as a backup, which applied to 9 clusters in the master table. Since the van den Bergh only gave values for NGC cluster, I chose to ignore other clusters such as Pal5 and Terzan7, as age and metallicity values were not given. I learned the important skill of reorganising astronomical data and cross matching catalogues. Since the rest of the analysis also depended on this table, I learned the importance of documenting data decisions clearly so my teammates can use them. What I would do differently next time is validate the merge sooner. After merging, 53 Harris clusters had no van den Bergh data, which was expected as van den Bergh covers fewer clusters. However, 3 van den Bergh clusters had no Harris match, which suggested a naming mismatch rather than missing data. Matching these three Nans inflated the merged table. This was confirmed by looking at the size of the table and creating a function make_label to prevent this issue.
