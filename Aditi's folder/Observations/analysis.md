Milky way Globular clusters: Metallicity vs Absolute magnitude

From my graph I have created from the data my first observation will be that most globular clusters have a low metallicity. Most of the data points are focused between [Fe/H]=-2.3 and -0.3. This indicates that most of the globular clusters in this sample have a low iron abundance compared with the sun.

My second observation is that the absolute magnitudes vary. Most of the clusters have absolute magnitudes between approximately M_v=-5 and -9.  We can see there is a large variation in brightness even if the clusters have similar metallicities.

My last observation is that there is no strong obvious linear relation. The points are clearly scattered rather than forming a clear straight line. The clusters with similar metallicities can have different absolute magnitudes. This suggests that the metallicity alone does not give reason for their brightness.


From our observations we can now analyse that a more negative absolute magnitude means a brighter object. The graph shows that the clusters with the greatest brightness are spread across the low metallicity region. There is no pattern when increasing metallicity consistently makes clusters brighter or fainter.

We can also see there is one unusual outlier in the graph, where the metallicity is 4 and the absolute magnitude is -0.8. We can see that the point is further away from all the data.
